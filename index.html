About

The new AESZ database provides arithmetic, algebraic, and enumerative information that complements the list of one-parameter Calabi–Yau operators compiled by Gert Almkvist, Christian van Enckevort, Duco van Straten, and Wadim Zudilin [1, 2].

The main additions are Euler factors of the Hasse–Weil zeta function, calculated following [3] to identify rank-two attractor points and test Langlands-type conjectures. Further additions include rational and integral monodromy structures.

Related databases and software

Resources for the study of Calabi–Yau and Fano varieties.

Databases

  • Original AESZ database. The earlier database of Calabi–Yau differential operators.

  • L-functions and Modular Forms Database (LMFDB). Data on L-functions, modular forms, and related arithmetic objects.

  • Higher-genus invariants and Calabi–Yau data. Albrecht Klemm’s collection of data on Calabi–Yau threefolds and their enumerative invariants.

  • Quantum periods of four-dimensional Fano varieties. Data from Tom Coates and Alexander M. Kasprzyk.

  • Kreuzer–Skarke Calabi–Yau data. Max Kreuzer and Harald Skarke’s collections of 4,319 reflexive polytopes in dimension three and 473,800,776 in dimension four, including 30,108 distinct Hodge pairs for Calabi–Yau threefold hypersurface families.

Software tools

  • CYTools. Software from the Liam McAllister Group for studying Calabi–Yau manifolds, including triangulations and intersection numbers.

  • ore_algebra. Tools for analytic continuation of solutions of differential equations and computation of monodromy matrices.
